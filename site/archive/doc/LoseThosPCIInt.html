LoseThos Interrupts

There are 16 ISA bus interrupts.  LoseThos assigns them to system vectors 
0x20-0x2F.  See IntsInit.  LoseThos uses timer#0, keyboard#1 and mouse#12.  Some 
system's mice refuse to generate interrupts, so LoseThos falls-back on polling.  
I, purposely, avoid interrupts for drives because they are a potential source of 
incompatibility, and LoseThos must maintain its distinctive simpleness.

There are 4 PCI interrupts per PCI bus, known as A,B,C and D.  LoseThos does not 
support PCI devices except HD Audio, which it polls.  The 4 PCI bus interrupts 
are shared by devices on a single PCI bus.  You must support all devices on a 
bus, or none, it seems.  If you support one device, you can't be sure it's not 
sharing an interrupt with another device.  If you have two devices with a shared 
interrupt, one you support and one you don't, you'll have problems if you don't 
acknowledge an interrupt from the device you don't support.  I hear they're 
edge-triggered, though, so maybe not.  I'm not an expert.

I think most computers have one of the Intel ICH chips, so I could probably have 
pretty wide compatibility if I assumed people had an ICH chip in bus#0.  The ICH 
chip versions differ, though, and I really don't want to get in the business of 
having redundant drivers for all ICH chips.  I prefer limiting LoseThos to least 
(greatest) common denominator hardware.  HD Audio is the only exception, but I 
hope that becomes standard.

PCI interrupts are shared between devices on ONE bus.  Therefore, since ICH 
chips occupy a whole single bus, usually bus #0, my concern about shared 
interrupts would involve only devices on an ICH chip and not all PCI devices on 
other PCI busses on the machine.  I could manage to resolve sharing for a known 
ICH device, but I'd still be forced to deal with all the different versions of 
ICH chips.

A keyboard or mouse only needs to be polled once every few milliseconds, if you 
are not using interrupts.  That's just reading a port.  An audio device with a 
big buffer, is similarly slow, so you can poll.  Disk access when you have 
nothing better to do with the focus task on a home system is okay to poll.  It 
is in the foreground.  Networks, however, must happen in the background and can 
be high bandwidth.  If they have big buffers, you might poll.  In the age of 
multicored CPU's, you could eliminate interrupts and DMA all together and 
dedicate cores to various I/O tasks.  It's probably better doing it normally, 
but I have compatibility to consider.

LoseThos uses cooperative multitasking when polling during disk activity.  The 
task swaps-in, reads the disk status port and yields to the next task.  Swapping 
in and out takes half a microsecond, actually less time than the microsecond 
needed for reading an ISA I/O port.  Other task CPU activity can take place 
while this is happening, but not too many or the disk task will poll too slowly. 
 If you notice multitasking issues when two tasks are accessing the disk, it's 
because one locks the disk during big file operation.  It does not break disk 
requests into pieces to share the drive.  See ::/LT/Doc/Lectures/Spinning.CPZ.
