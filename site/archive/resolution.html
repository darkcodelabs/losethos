The LoseThos 64-bit PC Operating System
x86_64, preemptive multitasking, multicore, open source
Without graphic acceleration, CPU load increases proportionately with the memory that has
to be updated.  So, 1600x1200x24 bit takes 37 times the processing power as 640x480x4.
It's a no brainer that the main thing keeping people from using LoseThos is the graphics.  I
did play around with other graphics modes and instead of my normal 4% CPU idle load, I
had like 40% idle CPU load.  I could conceal the issue by only updating what has changed,
but that would become apparent with full screen 60Hz games.  The fact that I struggle using
8 cores to do a 12 frame per second flight simulator at 640x480 pretty-much settles the
issue!  (I could combine pixel-sized polygons in the distance into combined polygons, but I
don't want to make the demo too complex.)  No way in hell am I trying for higher resolutions.
With that settled, I have been able to focus my attention on other things.

There are many benefits to having just 640x480.  You can write your programs for a fixed
size screen without worrying how it will appear.  You aren't as stressed over CPU load.  You
can use multicore to help update the screen, since it's not based on graphics acceleration.  
Graphics take very little room, little time to load from disk, little space on disk and less time
to upload.

Graphic art is kind-of a tedious pain in the neck for most people who write games
themselves.  After a few minutes of struggling to make images, I say, "Screw-it, I'm a
programmer not an artist."  My art sucks and it has nothing to do with the graphics modes.
Most amateur game makers would be far happier with LoseThos than they realize, but it's
depressing with no hope of commercial success for a game.
